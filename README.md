# A global assessment of public engagement, education, and outreach in marine protected area management plans

## Abstract

Public engagement is increasingly recognized as an important component of biodiversity conservation, with protected areas serving as platforms for education, participation, and stewardship. However, there is still limited systematic understanding of how public engagement is incorporated into conservation planning and management across diverse contexts. Here we present the first global, multilingual structured content analysis of public engagement in marine protected area (MPA) management plans. Across 172 management plans encompassing 555 MPAs in 39 sovereign states, 10 non-sovereign or special geographic entries, and one Area Beyond National Jurisdiction, we found that public engagement was widely embedded within management plans (86.6%, n = 149 discussed at least one public engagement action), with actions targeting schools, students, and educators described in 70.5% of eligible plans (n = 105). Although a strong majority of plans (77.9%, n = 116) described intended outcomes of engagement actions, fewer than one third (30.2%, n = 45) included explicit indicators or mechanisms to assess whether or to what extent these outcomes were achieved. The limited articulation of assessment approaches linked to intended outcomes may constrain opportunities for reflection, adaptive learning, and knowledge exchange across MPAs. Our findings highlight the growing integration of public engagement within marine protected area management while identifying opportunities to strengthen how engagement efforts are specified, assessed, and supported in practice. Strengthening the visibility and evaluation of engagement efforts may help recognize protected areas’ societal contributions and foster learning across diverse MPA contexts, supporting more effective biodiversity conservation.
